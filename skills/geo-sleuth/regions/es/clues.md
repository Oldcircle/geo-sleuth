# Spain clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Spain is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and signs

### Spanish words, and the co-official languages
- Look for: Spanish on shop fronts, station boards and official signs: `Salida` (exit), `Entrada` (entrance), `Estación` (station), `Farmacia`, `Ayuntamiento` (town hall), `Abierto`/`Cerrado`.
- Points to: Spain; the Spanish vocabulary is the strongest country-level text clue.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the region once a co-official language appears.
- Counterexamples: most of Latin America uses the same words, so the Spanish text alone does not separate Spain from the Americas. Catalan (`Sortida`), Basque (`Irteera`) or Galician (`Saída`) signs point to their communities.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Languages of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Spain) describes the co-official languages.

### Four digits then three letters
- Look for: a white plate with black characters and a blue EU band with `E`, in the format `1234 ABC` (four digits, then three letters), with no province code.
- Points to: Spain as the issuing country; the format is national, so the plate does not identify the province.
- Strength: strong for the country (single source); useless for the region.
- Counterexamples: Portugal uses a similar white plate but with a `P` band and a different letter grouping; a rental car can carry a foreign plate.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Spain) documents the current series.

## Addresses and numbers

### Five-digit postal code before the town name
- Look for: a five-digit code written before the town, for example `28001 Madrid`, `08001 Barcelona` or `41001 Sevilla`; the first two digits are the province.
- Points to: Spain and the province; the code groups the country from north (`01xxx` Álava) to south (`41xxx` Sevilla) and the islands (`07xxx`, `35xxx`, `38xxx`).
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: France, Italy and Germany also use five digits, so the code needs Spanish text or a Spanish town name next to it.
- Sources: [Correos](https://www.correos.es/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Nine-digit numbers with `+34`
- Look for: phone numbers written as nine digits (`91` Madrid, `93` Barcelona, `95` Sevilla/Málaga, `96` Valencia) and mobile numbers starting `6` or `7`, or the country code `+34` abroad.
- Points to: Spain; the two-digit area code and the nine-digit length are Spanish.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: Portugal uses nine digits too but with `+351` and different prefixes; a `+34` number can be printed on material produced elsewhere.
- Sources: [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`AP`/`N` network
- Look for: blue direction signs, `A-` autovía/autopista numbers (`A-1` to `A-7`, `A-92`), toll `AP-` numbers, and `N-` national roads.
- Points to: Spain; the `A`/`AP`/`N` numbering with blue signs is Spanish.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the province.
- Counterexamples: France and Portugal also use `A` numbers and blue signs, and regional roads use other prefixes (`C-` in Catalonia, `CV-` in Valencia). A route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Roads in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_Spain) lists the network; [DGT](https://www.dgt.es/) is the traffic authority.

### Renfe trains and the supermarket chains
- Look for: `Renfe` rail vehicles and station signage, `AVE` high-speed trains or `Cercanías` commuter branding, or the chains `Mercadona`, `Carrefour` and `El Corte Inglés`.
- Points to: Spain; the Renfe brands and the Spanish grocery chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the province.
- Counterexamples: Renfe runs some cross-border services, and Carrefour operates in several countries with local branding.
- Sources: [Renfe](https://www.renfe.com/) publishes its network; [Mercadona](https://www.mercadona.es/) lists its stores.
