# United Kingdom clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after the United Kingdom is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### English words, with Welsh or Gaelic alongside
- Look for: English on signs (`Way out`, `Exit`, `Station`, `Pharmacy`, `Council`, `Open`/`Closed`), and in Wales bilingual Welsh/English pairs such as `Araf/Slow` or `Ysbyty/Hospital`; in the Scottish Highlands Gaelic may appear on some signs.
- Points to: the United Kingdom; the bilingual Welsh pair is a strong Wales signal, and the English wording alone places it in the UK rather than Ireland or North America.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for Wales when Welsh appears.
- Counterexamples: Ireland also uses English on most signs, but with Irish place names and `km/h`-style signs in Irish; Australia, Canada and the US share the English wording. The Welsh pair is the cleanest separator.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Languages of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_United_Kingdom) describes the bilingual signage.

### White front plate, yellow rear plate
- Look for: a white plate on the front and a yellow plate on the rear, in the format `AB12 CDE` (two letters, two digits, three letters), with no region code; newer plates may carry a `UK` band instead of the EU stars.
- Points to: the United Kingdom as the issuing country; the front/rear colour difference is the clearest national plate marker.
- Strength: strong for the country (single source); the first letters group the registration office, but they do not identify the current location.
- Counterexamples: Ireland also uses white plates with black characters, and the Isle of Man, Jersey and Guernsey issue their own plates. A rental can carry a plate from another region.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_the_United_Kingdom) documents the current series and the area codes.

## Addresses and numbers

### Alphanumeric postcode with a space
- Look for: an outward code and an inward code separated by a space, for example `SW1A 1AA`, `M1 1AE` or `EH1 1YZ`, on mailboxes, forms and shop windows.
- Points to: the United Kingdom; the letter-digit pattern is British and Irish, and the outward letters narrow the area (`SW` London, `M` Manchester, `EH` Edinburgh, `CF` Cardiff, `BT` Belfast).
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the area.
- Counterexamples: Canada and the Netherlands also use alphanumeric postcodes, but with different patterns (`K1A 0B1`, `1234 AB`). The code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Sources: [Royal Mail](https://www.royalmail.com/) publishes the postcode format and directory.

### `+44` and area codes with a leading zero
- Look for: phone numbers written with a leading zero (`020` London, `0161` Manchester, `0131` Edinburgh, `0121` Birmingham) and mobile numbers starting `07`, or the country code `+44` abroad.
- Points to: the United Kingdom; the leading zero is dropped only with the country code.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: Ireland uses `+353` with different area codes; a `+44` number can appear on material produced elsewhere.
- Sources: [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `M`/`A` network
- Look for: blue direction signs on motorways (`M1`, `M25`, `M6`), green or white signs on `A` roads, roundabout diagrams on approach signs, and left-hand traffic.
- Points to: the United Kingdom; the `M` motorway numbering with blue signs and roundabout signage is British.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the region.
- Counterexamples: Ireland also drives on the left and uses `M` motorways with blue signs; Australia and New Zealand drive on the left with different road numbering. A route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [National Highways](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/) operates the motorways in England; [Wikipedia: Motorways in the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways_in_the_United_Kingdom) lists the network.

### National Rail and the high-street chains
- Look for: the red double-arrow `National Rail` symbol on station signs and trains, operator names such as `Great Western Railway`, `LNER` or `ScotRail`, or the chains `Tesco`, `Sainsbury's`, `Greggs` and `Boots`.
- Points to: the United Kingdom; the rail symbol and the British chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the region.
- Counterexamples: Tesco operates in Ireland and central Europe under the same brand, and rail operators run cross-border services to Ireland only via ferry connections.
- Sources: [National Rail](https://www.nationalrail.co.uk/) publishes the network; [Tesco](https://www.tesco.com/) lists its stores.
