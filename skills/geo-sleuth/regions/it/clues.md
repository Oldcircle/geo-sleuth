# Italy clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Italy is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### Italian words on fixed signs
- Look for: Italian on shop fronts, station boards and official signs: `Uscita` (exit), `Ingresso` (entrance), `Stazione` (station), `Farmacia`, `Comune`, `Chiuso` (closed), `Aperto` (open).
- Points to: Italy; the Italian vocabulary is the strongest country-level text clue.
- Strength: strong for the country; weak for the region, since standard Italian is uniform.
- Counterexamples: Switzerland's Ticino and parts of Switzerland use Italian, and San Marino and Vatican City are Italian-speaking enclaves. Slovenian and Croatian signs on the Adriatic coast are a different language.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Italian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_language) describes the distribution.

### Two letters for the province, then digits
- Look for: a white plate with black characters and a blue EU band with `I`, in the format `AB 123 CD`, where the first two letters name the province (`RM` Rome, `MI` Milan, `TO` Turin, `NA` Naples, `FI` Florence, `BO` Bologna, `VE` Venice, `GE` Genoa, `PA` Palermo, `BA` Bari).
- Points to: the province of registration; the first pair of letters is the province code, not a region.
- Strength: medium (single source); stale plates from before a province change keep the old code.
- Counterexamples: the code is where the vehicle was registered, not where the photo was taken, and a rental can carry a plate from another province. Vatican and diplomatic plates use other formats.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Italy) lists the province codes and the current format.

## Addresses and numbers

### Five-digit postal code before the town name
- Look for: a five-digit code written before the town, for example `00100 Roma`, `20121 Milano` or `50122 Firenze`, on mailboxes, forms and shop windows.
- Points to: Italy; the first digits group the country from north (`10xxx`, `20xxx`) to south (`80xxx`, `90xxx`).
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: France, Germany and Spain also use five digits, so the code needs Italian text or an Italian town name next to it.
- Sources: [Poste Italiane](https://www.poste.it/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Area codes without a leading zero, and `+39`
- Look for: phone numbers written without a leading zero (`06` Rome, `02` Milan, `011` Turin, `055` Florence, `081` Naples) and mobile numbers starting `3`, or the country code `+39` abroad.
- Points to: Italy; the fixed-line code is written without the trunk prefix, unlike France, Germany and Spain.
- Strength: medium (single source); the number format is a strong local marker.
- Counterexamples: `+39` also covers San Marino and Vatican City, and mobile numbers are portable across the country.
- Sources: [AGCOM](https://www.agcom.it/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Green motorway signs and the `A` network
- Look for: green direction signs on motorways (`A1` Autostrada del Sole, `A4` Turin–Trieste, `A14` Adriatic), `SS` and `SP` numbers on ordinary roads, and `Tangenziale` signs around cities.
- Points to: Italy; the green motorway signage, the `A` numbering and the `Tangenziale` naming are Italian.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the province.
- Counterexamples: Switzerland and France also use motorway numbers and green or blue signs; a route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Autostrade per l'Italia](https://www.autostrade.it/) operates most of the network; [Wikipedia: Autostrade of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrade_of_Italy) lists it.

### Trenitalia and the high-speed brands
- Look for: red-and-white `Trenitalia` rail vehicles and station signage, the `Frecciarossa`/`Frecciargento`/`Frecciabianca` brands, or the competing `Italo` trains.
- Points to: Italy; the two high-speed brands and the `Trenitalia` network are Italian.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the province.
- Counterexamples: Trenitalia runs cross-border services to France, Switzerland, Austria and Slovenia, and Italo is owned by a different company. A train can be photographed at a foreign station.
- Sources: [Trenitalia](https://www.trenitalia.com/) and [Italo](https://www.italotreno.com/) publish their networks.
