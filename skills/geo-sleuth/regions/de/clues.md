# Germany clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Germany is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### German words on fixed signs
- Look for: German on shop fronts, station boards and official signs: `Ausfahrt` (motorway exit), `Ausgang` (exit), `Eingang` (entrance), `Bahnhof` (station), `Apotheke`, `Rathaus` (town hall), `Geöffnet`/`Geschlossen`.
- Points to: Germany; the vocabulary is the strongest country-level text clue.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the region, since standard German is uniform.
- Counterexamples: Austria and German-speaking Switzerland use the same words; Austrian specifics such as `Jänner` or `Erdäpfel`, or Swiss `Trottoir`, point to those countries. A single sign cannot separate Germany from Austria.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: German language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) describes the distribution.

### White plate with a city code and a seasonal suffix
- Look for: a white plate with black characters and a blue EU band with `D`, opening with one to three letters for the registration district (`B` Berlin, `M` Munich, `HH` Hamburg, `K` Cologne, `F` Frankfurt, `S` Stuttgart), followed by digits; a trailing `H` marks historic vehicles and `E` electric ones.
- Points to: the district of registration; the opening letters are the city or district code.
- Strength: medium (single source); the plate confirms Germany and narrows the district.
- Counterexamples: the code is where the vehicle was registered, not where the photo was taken, and a rental can carry a plate from another district. Austrian plates use a different layout.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Germany) documents the district codes and the seasonal suffixes.

## Addresses and numbers

### Five-digit postal code before the town name
- Look for: a five-digit code written before the town, for example `10115 Berlin`, `80331 München` or `20095 Hamburg`.
- Points to: Germany; the code system was introduced in 1993 and covers the country with `0xxxx` in the east and `9xxxx` in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg.
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: France, Italy and Spain also use five digits, so the code needs German text or a German town name next to it.
- Sources: [Deutsche Post](https://www.deutschepost.de/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Area codes with a leading zero, and `+49`
- Look for: phone numbers written with a leading zero (`030` Berlin, `089` Munich, `040` Hamburg, `0221` Cologne, `0711` Stuttgart) and mobile numbers starting `015`/`016`/`017`, or the country code `+49` abroad.
- Points to: Germany; the leading zero is dropped only with the country code.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: Austria uses `+43` with similar-looking area codes (`01` Vienna); Switzerland uses `+41`. Only the country code or the specific area code separates them.
- Sources: [Bundesnetzagentur](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`B` network
- Look for: blue direction signs on motorways (`A1`, `A3`, `A9`), yellow signs on `B` federal roads, `Umleitung` diversion signs, and `Umweltzone` environmental-zone stickers on windscreens.
- Points to: Germany; the blue `A`/yellow `B` signage and the environmental-zone system are German.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the province.
- Counterexamples: Austria also uses blue motorway signs and `A`/`B` numbering, and the Czech Republic uses similar signage. A route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Autobahn GmbH](https://www.autobahn.de/) operates the motorways; [Wikipedia: Autobahns of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobahns_of_Germany) lists the network.

### DB trains and the discount supermarket chains
- Look for: red `DB` rail vehicles and station signage, `ICE`/`IC` train branding, or the chains `Aldi`, `Lidl`, `Rewe`, `Edeka` and `dm`.
- Points to: Germany; the DB brand and the German discount chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the province.
- Counterexamples: Aldi and Lidl operate across Europe under the same brands, and DB runs cross-border services to Austria, Switzerland, France and the Czech Republic.
- Sources: [Deutsche Bahn](https://www.bahn.de/) publishes its network; [Aldi](https://www.aldi.de/) and [Lidl](https://www.lidl.de/) list their stores.
