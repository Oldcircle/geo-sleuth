# France clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after France is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### French words on fixed signs
- Look for: French on shop fronts, station boards and official signs: `Sortie` (exit), `Entrée` (entrance), `Gare` (station), `Pharmacie`, `Mairie` (town hall), `Ouvert`/`Fermé`.
- Points to: France; the vocabulary is the strongest country-level text clue.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the region once a regional language appears.
- Counterexamples: Belgium, Switzerland and Quebec also use French. Belgian `Bruxelles/Brussel` pairs and Swiss `Genève` signs, or Quebec's `arrêt` signs, point elsewhere; the `Mairie`/`Gare` wording alone is not enough.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: French language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) describes the distribution.

### Two letters, three digits, two letters
- Look for: a white plate with black characters and a blue EU band with `F`, in the format `AB-123-CD` (two letters, three digits, two letters), with no region code since 2009.
- Points to: France as the issuing country; the format is national, so the plate does not identify the region.
- Strength: strong for the country (single source); useless for the region.
- Counterexamples: Italy uses a similar `AB 123 CD` shape but with a different band and no hyphens; a rental car can carry a foreign plate.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_France) documents the current SIV series.

## Addresses and numbers

### Five-digit postal code before the town name
- Look for: a five-digit code written before the town, for example `75001 Paris`, `13001 Marseille` or `31000 Toulouse`; the first two digits are the département.
- Points to: France and the département; the code groups the country from `01xxx` in the Ain to `95xxx` around Paris, with overseas codes in the `97xxx` range.
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: Italy, Germany and Spain also use five digits, so the code needs French text or a French town name next to it.
- Sources: [La Poste](https://www.laposte.fr/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### `+33` and area codes written without the trunk zero
- Look for: numbers written as `+33 1 23 45 67 89` or with a leading zero (`01` Paris, `04` Marseille/Lyon, `05` Bordeaux/Toulouse), and mobiles starting `06` or `07`.
- Points to: France; the trunk zero is dropped with the country code, and the `06`/`07` mobile ranges are French.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: Belgium uses `+32` and Switzerland `+41` with similar-looking area codes; only the country code or the specific area code separates them.
- Sources: [ARCEP](https://www.arcep.fr/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`N`/`D` network
- Look for: blue direction signs on autoroutes (`A1`, `A7`, `A9`), `N` national and `D` departmental road numbers, `Péage` toll signs, and roundabouts with `Cédez le passage` markings.
- Points to: France; the `A`/`N`/`D` hierarchy with `Péage` signage is French.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the region.
- Counterexamples: Italy and Spain also use `A` motorway numbers, and Belgium uses blue signs. A route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Roads in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roads_in_France) lists the network; [Vinci Autoroutes](https://www.vinci-autoroutes.com/) operates part of it.

### SNCF trains and the supermarket chains
- Look for: `SNCF` rail vehicles and station signage, `TGV`/`TER` branding, or the chains `Carrefour`, `E.Leclerc`, `Lidl` and `Fnac`.
- Points to: France; the SNCF brands and the French retail chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the region.
- Counterexamples: Carrefour and Lidl operate across Europe under the same brands, and SNCF runs cross-border services to Belgium, Germany, Switzerland, Italy and Spain.
- Sources: [SNCF](https://www.sncf.com/) publishes its network; [Carrefour](https://www.carrefour.fr/) lists its stores.
