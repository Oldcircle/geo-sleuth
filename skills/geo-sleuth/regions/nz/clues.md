# New Zealand clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after New Zealand is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-16. Public transport branding narrows a search, but a vehicle can travel outside its normal service area: corroborate with fixed signage or another independent clue.

## Public transport

### Auckland Link bus names and liveries
- Look for: a bus with readable Link lettering in the matching colour: CityLink bright red, InnerLink bright green, OuterLink orange, TāmakiLink bright blue. Match the service name as well as the colour.
- Points to: Auckland. CityLink: the city centre (Wynyard Quarter, Queen Street, Karangahape Road). InnerLink: the inner city and nearest suburbs (Parnell, Newmarket, Ponsonby Road). OuterLink: the inner suburbs, Newmarket to St Lukes through the city centre, Parnell, Herne Bay, Westmere, Point Chevalier and Mount Albert. TāmakiLink: Tāmaki Drive from Waitematā Station through Mission Bay and Saint Heliers to Glen Innes. The brand alone does not identify a particular stop.
- Strength: medium (single source); needs another local clue. Colour without readable service text is weak.
- Counterexamples: other buses can be red, green, orange or blue; generic "Link" text is insufficient. Routes change: since 17 November 2024 OuterLink no longer runs through Epsom, Mount Eden and Balmoral, so older imagery can show it there. Out-of-service or transferred vehicles can be away from their usual routes, and replacement buses may lack the expected livery.
- Sources: [Auckland Transport: Link bus service](https://at.govt.nz/bus-train-ferry/bus-services/link-bus-service) describes the four Link colours, their routes and the 2024 OuterLink change.

### Metlink public transport branding
- Look for: the readable name "Metlink" on a public transport vehicle, stop sign, station sign or posted timetable; prefer fixed passenger information to an advertisement or a handheld ticket.
- Points to: the Greater Wellington transport network, including Wellington City, Hutt Valley, Porirua, Kāpiti Coast and Wairarapa. Keep the wider region in play rather than assuming Wellington City.
- Strength: medium (single source); corroborate with a stop name or an independent local clue.
- Counterexamples: the same brand covers several towns and transport modes. A vehicle away from its route, or a portable ticket or promotional item, need not be in its service area; branding alone cannot distinguish the region's cities.
- Sources: [Greater Wellington: Metlink bus, train and ferry](https://www.gw.govt.nz/transport/metlink-bus-train-and-ferry/) defines the network's coverage and its responsibility for stops, stations and signage.

### Orbus public transport branding
- Look for: readable "Orbus" branding on a bus or passenger information at a bus stop, ideally with a route destination visible.
- Points to: Otago's Orbus service areas: the Dunedin network and its outlying routes, or the Queenstown network. Do not choose between them from the brand alone; the Dunedin network also reaches Ōamaru from 1 October 2026.
- Strength: medium (single source: Otago Regional Council); needs route text or another independent clue to narrow further.
- Counterexamples: Orbus is shared by two networks, including places beyond the central cities such as Mosgiel and Arrowtown, and the Ōamaru extension. A depot transfer or out-of-service vehicle may be elsewhere. A route destination names where the bus is going, not necessarily where the photo was taken.
- Sources: [Otago Regional Council: About Orbus](https://www.orc.govt.nz/orbus/travel-with-us/about-orbus) describes both networks and their outlying services; [Route 1 changes](https://www.orc.govt.nz/orbus/dunedin-bus-timetables/dunedin-bus-changes-2026/route-1-changes) documents the Ōamaru extension and start date.

### MyWay by Metro on-demand transport
- Look for: the complete readable brand "MyWay by Metro" on a passenger vehicle or local service information. Do not infer the location from the word "Metro" alone.
- Points to: Timaru's urban service area in Canterbury, including Washdyke, Gleniti, Redruth and the Port; the brand does not identify a fixed route or neighbourhood.
- Strength: medium (single source); corroborate with fixed local signage or another independent clue.
- Counterexamples: on-demand pickup points vary, so the vehicle does not imply a scheduled bus stop. A relocated vehicle or portable promotional material can be outside Timaru; partial "Metro" branding is not specific enough.
- Sources: [Metro Timaru: MyWay by Metro](https://timaru.metroinfo.co.nz/myway-by-metro/) identifies the service area and explains its variable pickup and drop-off points.
