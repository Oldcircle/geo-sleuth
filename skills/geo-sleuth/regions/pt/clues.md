# Portugal clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Portugal is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### Portuguese spelling on fixed signs
- Look for: Portuguese words with their characteristic endings and diacritics on shop fronts, street plates and notices: `Saída`, `Entrada`, `Farmácia`, `Padaria`, `Câmara Municipal`, `Junta de Freguesia`, `Bombeiros`.
- Points to: Portugal (country-level confirmation); the vocabulary of the civil parish (`freguesia`) and municipality (`concelho`) is specific to Portuguese administration.
- Strength: strong for the country; weak for any region.
- Counterexamples: Brazil uses Portuguese too, but with different vocabulary and spelling (`farmácia`/`farmácia`, `ônibus` vs `autocarro`); Galician and Spanish signage in border areas is close enough to misread. English or Spanish-only signs appear in tourist areas.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Portuguese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) covers the variants; [gov.pt](https://www.gov.pt/) is the official portal.

### Two-letter, two-digit, two-letter plate
- Look for: a white plate with black characters, a blue band with the EU stars and `P` on the left, and the format `AA-00-AA` (since 2020) or the older `00-AA-00`.
- Points to: Portugal as the issuing country; the plate carries no region code, so it cannot narrow the district.
- Strength: strong for the country (single source); useless for a region.
- Counterexamples: trailers, mopeds and agricultural vehicles use other formats; older plates remain valid, so a `00-AA-00` plate is still Portuguese. A foreign plate in a rental car says nothing about the photo's location.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Portugal) documents both series and the EU band.

## Addresses and numbers

### Four digits, a hyphen, three digits
- Look for: a postal code written `0000-000` before the town name, for example `1000-001 Lisboa` or `4000-007 Porto`, on mailboxes, vans, shop fronts and invoices.
- Points to: Portugal; the first digit groups the country, and `9xxx` covers the Atlantic archipelagos of Madeira and the Azores.
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: Spain uses five digits, so a five-digit code next to Portuguese text points across the border. A code can be printed on a mail piece handled elsewhere.
- Sources: [CTT](https://www.ctt.pt/) publishes the postal-code directory and the `0000-000` format.

### Nine-digit phone numbers without a trunk prefix
- Look for: phone numbers written as nine digits, usually starting `2` (landline) or `9` (mobile), sometimes with the country code `+351`.
- Points to: Portugal; there is no leading trunk zero, while Spanish numbers also have nine digits (landlines start `9`, mobiles `6` or `7`).
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: mobile numbers in Spain also start with 6 or 7 and have nine digits; only the `+351` or the local context separates them.
- Sources: [ANACOM](https://www.anacom.pt/) is the telecom regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and public services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`EN` road network
- Look for: blue direction signs on motorways (`A1`, `A2`, `A23` …), white-on-green or white-on-blue signs on national roads (`EN`/`N` numbers), and the yellow-and-green `Via Verde` toll gantries.
- Points to: Portugal; the `A`/`EN` numbering and the electronic toll system are Portuguese.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the district.
- Counterexamples: Spain uses `A-` and `N-` prefixes with a hyphen, and Spanish toll roads have their own transponders. A route number can appear on a map or a rental agreement photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Infraestruturas de Portugal](https://www.infraestruturasdeportugal.pt/) lists the network; [Via Verde](https://www.viaverde.pt/) describes the toll system.

### GNR and PSP vehicles, red Bombeiros trucks
- Look for: patrol cars marked `GNR` (dark green and white) in rural areas or `PSP` (white and blue) in cities, and red fire engines marked `Bombeiros`.
- Points to: Portugal; the two police forces split national and urban policing, and `Bombeiros` are the volunteer fire brigades.
- Strength: medium (single source); vehicle markings are readable in a street-level shot but easy to miss.
- Counterexamples: private security cars copy police liveries in some countries; a GNR vehicle can be photographed anywhere on a national road, so it does not localize beyond the country.
- Sources: [GNR](https://www.gnr.pt/) and [PSP](https://www.psp.pt/) describe their uniforms and vehicles.
