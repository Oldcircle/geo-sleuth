# Austria clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Austria is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### Austrian German vocabulary on fixed signs
- Look for: German with Austrian words and spellings: `Jänner` (January), `Erdäpfel` (potatoes), `Paradeiser` (tomatoes), `Topfen` (quark), `Semmel` (bread roll), `Gemeindeamt`, `Bezirkshauptmannschaft`, `Meldeamt`.
- Points to: Austria (country-level confirmation); the vocabulary separates it from Germany and German-speaking Switzerland.
- Strength: strong for the country; weak for any state.
- Counterexamples: Bavaria shares some kitchen words (`Semmel`, `Topfen`), and standard German dominates in news and chain-store signage. English-only signs appear in tourist centres.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Austrian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_German) lists the vocabulary; [oesterreich.gv.at](https://www.oesterreich.gv.at/) is the official portal.

### One district letter, digits, a final letter
- Look for: a white plate with black characters, a blue band with the EU stars and `A`, opening with a single letter for the registration district (`W` Vienna, `G` Graz, `L` Linz, `S` Salzburg, `I` Innsbruck), then digits and a trailing letter.
- Points to: the district of registration; the single opening letter is the district code, not a region name.
- Strength: medium (single source); the plate confirms Austria and narrows the district, but the vehicle can travel anywhere.
- Counterexamples: personalised and diplomatic plates use other shapes; the code is where the car was registered, not where the photo was taken, and a rental can be registered in another province.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Austria) documents the district codes and the format.

## Addresses and numbers

### Four-digit postal code before the town name
- Look for: a four-digit code written before the town, for example `1010 Wien`, `8010 Graz` or `6020 Innsbruck`, on mailboxes, forms and shop windows.
- Points to: Austria; the first digit groups the country from west (6xxx) to east (1xxx and 7xxx for Burgenland).
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: Germany also uses five digits, so a five-digit code next to German text points across the border; a code can be printed on a mail piece handled elsewhere.
- Sources: [Post AG](https://www.post.at/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Area codes with a leading zero, and `+43`
- Look for: phone numbers written with a leading zero (`01` Vienna, `0316` Graz, `0732` Linz, `0662` Salzburg, `0512` Innsbruck) and mobile numbers starting `06`, or the country code `+43` abroad.
- Points to: Austria; the leading zero is dropped only with the country code.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: Germany also uses leading-zero area codes (`030` Berlin, `089` Munich); only the `+43` or the specific code separates them.
- Sources: [RTR](https://www.rtr.at/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs, `A`/`B` road numbers and the vignette
- Look for: blue direction signs on motorways (`A1` Westautobahn, `A2` Südautobahn, `A9` Pyhrn), `B` numbers on federal roads, and windscreen vignettes or `ASFINAG` signage.
- Points to: Austria; the `A`/`B` numbering and the vignette system are Austrian.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the province.
- Counterexamples: Germany uses `A` numbers too (blue signs as well), and the Czech and Slovenian networks also sell vignettes. A road number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [ASFINAG](https://www.asfinag.at/) operates the motorways and the vignette; [Wikipedia: Autobahns of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobahns_of_Austria) lists the network.

### ÖBB trains and the supermarket chains
- Look for: red-and-white `ÖBB` rail vehicles and station signage, or the chains `Spar`, `Billa`, `Hofer` and `Bipa` on shop fronts and delivery vans.
- Points to: Austria; the chains are Austrian and their branding is widespread, including small towns.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the province.
- Counterexamples: Spar and Billa also operate in neighbouring countries, and ÖBB trains run to Germany, Italy and Hungary. Franchise holders can use the brand outside its home market.
- Sources: [ÖBB](https://www.oebb.at/) is the national rail operator; [Spar](https://www.spar.at/) and [Billa](https://www.billa.at/) publish their store networks.
