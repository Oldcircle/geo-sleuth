# Brazil clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Only clues whose "Points to" stays inside Brazil belong here; clues that tell Brazil apart from other countries belong in `references/clues/global.md`.

## Text and numbers

### Plate letters → state of first registration
- Look for: the three letters of a vehicle plate: grey with black characters for private vehicles or red with white characters for taxis, buses and other paid transport, `ABC-1234` (issued from 1990 until each state switched between September 2018 and February 2020), or the white Mercosul plate with the blue `BRASIL` band, `ABC1D23`
- Points to: the state (UF) where the vehicle was first registered: each Detran received its own letter blocks and the Mercosul plate kept them → `clues.py lookup plate <plate> --country br`, or `board.py apply --kind plate --value <plate> --lr 2` (the default `--lr 20` would treat it as strong; 3–5 only when most cars parked in a quiet street agree)
- Strength: weak for where the photo was taken; medium when most cars parked in a quiet residential street fall in one state's blocks
- Counterexamples: the letters never change when a vehicle moves to another state, so used cars carry their first state for life; about 70% of rental-company cars are registered in Minas Gerais, where registration is quick and cheap and two of the largest rental companies (Localiza, Unidas) have their head offices, and are driven everywhere; Uruguay's plates are also three letters and four digits and the lookup gives them a Brazilian state, so near the Uruguayan border check for a URUGUAY band first; trucks and coaches cross the country; a few blocks are split by their digits (SAV up to SAV-1000 in São Paulo, the rest in Ceará); series released after May 2014 come only from en.wikipedia's community table and are marked unverified, except Paraná's RHA–RHZ, SDP–SFO and TAI–TBZ, which Detran-PR announced; 16 combinations are disputed between two lists
- Sources: Detran-PR (Paraná's blocks AAA–BEZ from 1990, RHA–RHZ 2020, SDP–SFO 2022, TAI–TBZ 2024), https://www.detran.pr.gov.br/Noticia/Parana-recebe-nova-sequencia-de-placas-veiculares-com-439956-unidades-para-registro; Portal do Trânsito (each state keeps its own sequence on Mercosul plates), https://www.portaldotransito.com.br/noticias/fiscalizacao-e-legislacao/documentacao-do-veiculo/placas-dos-veiculos-sequencia-de-caracteres-ainda-define-de-qual-estado-vem-o-veiculo-veja-a-resposta/; carrosnors (letters and numbers stay when a car changes state; series as of May 2014), https://www.carrosnors.com.br/inicio-de-placas-de-veiculos-por-estado/; en.wikipedia "Vehicle registration plates of Brazil" (blocks per state; plate colours), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Brazil; pt.wikipedia "Placas de identificação de veículos no Brasil" (start date per state, the last in February 2020; red commercial plates; the SAV split), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placas_de_identificação_de_veículos_no_Brasil; Diário do Comércio, February 2023 (70% of rental cars registered in Minas Gerais), https://diariodocomercio.com.br/economia/por-que-70-dos-carros-de-locadoras-no-brasil-sao-de-minas-gerais/; en.wikipedia "Vehicle registration plates of Uruguay" (national ABC 1234 format), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Uruguay

### Grey plate strip: state and town of registration
- Look for: on a grey or red plate (`ABC-1234`), the narrow metal strip above the characters with the state abbreviation and the municipality name (`<UF>-<MUNICÍPIO>`)
- Points to: the municipality where the vehicle is registered → add it as a candidate; `clues.py lookup admin "<município>" --country br` gives its state. The strip used to be swapped when the owner moved town; since each state adopted Mercosul plates (September 2018–February 2020), a move to another municipality or state requires a Mercosul plate, so a grey plate still in use shows a town where the car has been registered since at least then
- Strength: medium for a car parked in a residential street, more when several parked cars show the same town; weak on highways and in car parks
- Counterexamples: the strip is small and often unreadable at Street View resolution; rental and company fleets register where their head office is (most rental cars carry Minas Gerais registrations); Mercosul plates have no strip, so a recent car says nothing about its town, except Rio de Janeiro's first Mercosul plates of 2018 (next entry)
- Sources: pt.wikipedia "Placas de identificação de veículos no Brasil" (metal strip with UF and municipality, swapped on a move between municipalities under the old system), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placas_de_identificação_de_veículos_no_Brasil; carrosnors (only the detachable strip with town and UF changes when a car moves), https://www.carrosnors.com.br/inicio-de-placas-de-veiculos-por-estado/; CONTRAN Resolution 969/2022, art. 56 §1 (a vehicle already on the road gets the Mercosul plate on a change of municipality or state), https://www.detran.pr.gov.br/sites/default/arquivos_restritos/files/documento/2022-07/resolucao_969_2022-contran.pdf; Diário do Comércio, February 2023 (rental fleets registered in Minas Gerais), https://diariodocomercio.com.br/economia/por-que-70-dos-carros-de-locadoras-no-brasil-sao-de-minas-gerais/

### Mercosul plate with state and town emblems
- Look for: a white Mercosul plate (`ABC1D23`) with two small emblems at its right end, the state's and the municipality's, with the town's name
- Points to: a Rio de Janeiro registration from 11 September to late November or early December 2018: Rio was the first and, until December 2018, the only state issuing Mercosul plates, and CONTRAN Resolution 748 (dated 30 November, in force from its publication on 3 December 2018) struck the emblem items from the plate design while letting plates already issued stay; read the town name directly
- Strength: strong for a Rio de Janeiro registration; for the photo's place, as weak or strong as any registration (see the grey-plate strip)
- Counterexamples: it marks where the car was registered in 2018, not where it is now; every later Mercosul plate is blank there, so a plate without emblems says nothing
- Sources: Serpro, 11 September 2018 (Detran-RJ first to issue the new plate; state and municipal arms on the right side), https://serpro.gov.br/menu/noticias/noticias-2018/placa-mercosul-comeca-a-ser-adotada-no-pais; CONTRAN Resolution 748/2018 (revokes items 1.7–1.8.1 of Annex I of Resolution 729; issued plates need not be replaced; schedule for the other states from December 2018), https://www.gov.br/transportes/pt-br/assuntos/transito/conteudo-contran/resolucoes/resolucao7482018.pdf; Normas Brasil (Resolution 748 published in the Diário Oficial on 3 December 2018), https://www.normasbrasil.com.br/norma/resolucao-748-2018_370066.html; pt.wikipedia "Placas de identificação de veículos no Brasil" (start date per state; Resolution 748 removed state flags and municipal arms), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placas_de_identificação_de_veículos_no_Brasil

### Phone number with a DDD on a sign
- Look for: a phone number on a shop sign, awning, painted wall or van: `(DD) NNNN-NNNN` for a landline, `(DD) 9NNNN-NNNN` for a mobile (the ninth digit was added between 2012 and 2016); older signs write `(0xxDD)` or `(0DD)`
- Points to: the DDD area → `clues.py lookup area-code <number> --country br` (national or +55 form; `board.py apply --lr 2` for a mobile number): one state with the municipalities the code covers (count and largest towns); 61 is the Distrito Federal plus 12 towns of Goiás, and 42, 47 and 49 each take in one twin town across the Paraná–Santa Catarina line
- Strength: medium for a landline on a shop's own sign (a fixed line stays inside its área local, which since Anatel's 2026 reform is the whole DDD area); weak for a mobile number or a number on a vehicle
- Counterexamples: a mobile number keeps the DDD where the line was registered (portability works only within the same DDD) and goes wherever its owner goes; chains print a head-office number or a non-geographic number (0800, 0300, 3003-/4004-xxxx); a DDD covers 13 to 223 municipalities, so even a landline narrows to part of a state at best
- Sources: Anatel, Plano Geral de Códigos Nacionais (code per municipality, with IBGE codes; 2019 file, which a check of 26 border and Entorno towns against their current numbers in November 2026 found unchanged), https://www.anatel.gov.br/dadosabertos/PDA/Codigo_Nacional/PGCN.csv; Anatel FAQ on tariff areas, January 2026 (67 numbering areas = DDDs; from 2026 each local area equals a DDD area; the Brasília local area includes the Goiás towns on 61), https://sistemas.anatel.gov.br/anexar-api/publico/anexos/download/7c51a53e26edf77426cb85e42f1080de; Idec (mobile portability only within the same DDD), https://idec.org.br/consultas/dicas-e-direitos/entenda-a-portabilidade-numerica; en.wikipedia "Telephone numbers in Brazil" (number formats, carrier codes, ninth digit, non-geographic numbers), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_numbers_in_Brazil

### CEP (postal code) on a façade, plaque or sign
- Look for: an eight-digit code `NNNNN-NNN` on a shop sign, façade, building plaque, real-estate banner, or a street-name plate (São Paulo's plates and the newer concession plates of Recife and Porto Alegre print it)
- Points to: the state from the first digits → `clues.py lookup postal-code <cep> --country br`; the first digits also separate most capitals from the rest of their state, and the lookup says so (São Paulo city 01–05 and 080–084, Rio de Janeiro city 200–237, Belo Horizonte 300–319, Curitiba 800–829, Porto Alegre 900–919; capital ranges: single source); the other blocks are old mail-sorting regions, not today's metro areas
- Strength: strong for the state when the CEP is the building's own address; weak on vehicles, flyers and packaging
- Counterexamples: banners and vans print the head office's address; old plaques can carry pre-2008 codes: Rondônia used 789xx until August 2008 and has used 768xx–769xx since, a range that earlier belonged to Goiás; 10xxx is disputed between published tables (marked unverified)
- Sources: pt.wikipedia "Código de Endereçamento Postal" (ranges by state and capital; Rondônia's 2008 change), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Código_de_Endereçamento_Postal; ruacep (state ranges), https://www.ruacep.com.br/guias/postais/faixa-de-cep-por-estado/; Correios API manual (Acre 69900000–69999999), https://www.correios.com.br/atendimento/developers/manuais/manual-api-busca-cep; OpenStreetMap postal_code tags of 2,469 municipality relations (Overpass query of boundary=administrative, admin_level=8 in Brazil, 12 November 2026; all inside their state's range), https://overpass-api.de; CET-SP street-name plate standard (CEP printed on São Paulo plates), https://www.cetsp.com.br/media/1469459/PlacaIdentVLogradRev00.pdf

## Roads

### Federal highway shield with the state on top
- Look for: a white shield-shaped sign with black lettering: a narrow band at the top with a state abbreviation (`ES`, `CE`, …) above `BR` and three digits (`BR 101`); it stands after a federal road begins and after its main junctions
- Points to: the state written in the band, and the road → `osm.py along --bbox <state bbox> --line '["highway"]["ref"~"BR-101"]' --step 2000` to sample it
- Strength: medium (single source: DNIT's manual reuses CONTRAN's drawing)
- Counterexamples: none documented yet (single source), which is why it stays medium; the band is compulsory in CONTRAN's design, so a shield without one is non-standard and simply gives no state
- Sources: CONTRAN, Manual Brasileiro de Sinalização de Trânsito vol. III, 2022 edition (Annex III of Resolution 973/2022), §4.1.1 (the federal shield must carry the state abbreviation at the top; placement), https://www.gov.br/transportes/pt-br/assuntos/transito/arquivos-senatran/docs/copy_of___03___MBST_Vol._III___Sin._Vert._Indicacao.pdf; DNIT, 2016 instruction of the first BR-Legal programme (the same shield required at the start of federal roads), https://www.gov.br/dnit/pt-br/rodovias/operacoes-rodoviarias/programa-br-legal/manual_BRLEGAL.pdf

### State road prefix
- Look for: a road number with a state abbreviation in front, `SP-330`, `MG-050`, `PR-317`, `CE 251`, on a white pentagon marker, a kilometre plate or a direction sign
- Points to: that state; the road is run by the state
- Strength: strong
- Counterexamples: a state prefix with a federal number (`MGC-120`, `RSC-471`, `PRC-280`) is a state-run road on a federal route, planned or delegated: still that state, but read the number as a BR number; in 2009 Rio Grande do Sul's DAER said old signs would be replaced only as they wore out, so older-style signs may remain; a direction sign near a state line can point to the neighbouring state's roads (an `MG-` road signed from São Paulo), so take the state from markers and kilometre plates on the road itself
- Sources: CONTRAN, Manual Brasileiro de Sinalização de Trânsito vol. III, 2022 edition, §4.1.1 (state markers drawn as `CE 251`, `SP 55`), https://www.gov.br/transportes/pt-br/assuntos/transito/arquivos-senatran/docs/copy_of___03___MBST_Vol._III___Sin._Vert._Indicacao.pdf; en.wikipedia "Brazilian Highway System" (state highways numbered with the state abbreviation), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Highway_System; Plonkit Brazil guide (community; state abbreviations on kilometre plates), https://www.plonkit.net/brazil; Agência Minas (MGC roads), https://www.agenciaminas.mg.gov.br/noticia/entenda-os-criterios-para-definicao-dos-nomes-das-rodovias-estaduais-e-federais-120471; Rio Grande do Sul government, DAER nomenclature (RSC; sign replacement), https://www.estado.rs.gov.br/daer-padroniza-nomenclatura-de-rodovias; Agência Estadual de Notícias do Paraná (PRC-280), https://www.parana.pr.gov.br/aen/Noticia/Obra-emblematica-do-Sudoeste-restauracao-da-PRC-280-em-concreto-recebe-ultimos-ajustes

### State-specific road prefixes
- Look for: a road prefix that is more than the bare state abbreviation
- Points to: São Paulo: `SPA` access roads and `SPI` interconnections, written as `SPA-<km>/<parent road>`, `SPD` junctions, `SPM` side roads. Minas Gerais: `LMG` bypasses and links, `AMG` accesses (four digits, the first two the DER-MG district), `MGC` state-run stretches of federal routes (formerly `MGT`). Rio Grande do Sul: `ERS` state roads, `RSC` stretches that carry a federal number, `VRS` local roads. Distrito Federal: `DF-` numbers and the Estrada Parque acronyms (`EPIA`, `EPTG`, `EPNB`, …), which on their own point to Brasília
- Strength: medium (single source per state)
- Counterexamples: Minas Gerais and the Distrito Federal number their own roads by category around their capital with the same digit logic as BR roads, so don't read those numbers as BR bands; "Estrada Parque" written out also names roads elsewhere, such as MS-184 in the Pantanal
- Sources: São Paulo state Decree 49.476/2005 (SP numbering and SPA, SPI, SPD, SPM), https://www.al.sp.gov.br/repositorio/legislacao/decreto/2005/decreto-49476-11.03.2005.html; Agência Minas, DER-MG naming criteria (MG, LMG, AMG, MGC), https://www.agenciaminas.mg.gov.br/noticia/entenda-os-criterios-para-definicao-dos-nomes-das-rodovias-estaduais-e-federais-120471; Rio Grande do Sul government, DAER nomenclature (ERS, RSC, VRS), https://www.estado.rs.gov.br/daer-padroniza-nomenclatura-de-rodovias; DER-DF road-system presentation (DF numbering, Estradas Parque), https://der.df.gov.br/documents/d/der/apresentacao_da_legislacao_srdf_modificado_em_22_10_2024; Campo Grande News ("Estrada Parque" in the Pantanal), https://www.campograndenews.com.br/cidades/interior/passagem-de-caminhoes-esta-suspensa-na-estrada-parque

### BR number → direction and band
- Look for: the three digits of a federal road number on a shield, a kilometre plate or a direction sign
- Points to: the first digit gives the kind of road: `0xx` radial from Brasília; `1xx` north–south, the last two digits running from 00 in the far east through 50 on Brasília's meridian to 99 in the far west (a longitude band); `2xx` east–west, from 00 in the far north through 50 at Brasília's latitude to 99 in the far south (a latitude band); `3xx` diagonal; `4xx` link roads, where numbers from 50 up lie south of Brasília's latitude but low numbers do not reliably mean north. Then `osm.py along` on that road inside the band
- Strength: strong for the kind of road; medium for the band of a 1xx or 2xx road; weak for a low 4xx number
- Counterexamples: BR-418 runs from Teófilo Otoni to Caravelas, south of Brasília, despite its low number; where federal roads share a stretch it carries the lowest number; a long road wanders away from its nominal band; Minas Gerais and the Distrito Federal copy the scheme around their own capital
- Sources: DNIT, "Nomenclatura das rodovias federais" (categories, numbering rules, overlaps), https://www.gov.br/dnit/pt-br/rodovias/rodovias-federais/nomeclatura-das-rodovias-federais; en.wikipedia "Brazilian Highway System" (categories), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Highway_System; pt.wikipedia "Rodovia longitudinal", https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodovia_longitudinal; Agência Minas (restates the categories; MG scheme), https://www.agenciaminas.mg.gov.br/noticia/entenda-os-criterios-para-definicao-dos-nomes-das-rodovias-estaduais-e-federais-120471; DER-DF presentation (DF scheme), https://der.df.gov.br/documents/d/der/apresentacao_da_legislacao_srdf_modificado_em_22_10_2024; pt.wikipedia "BR-418" (route), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-418

### Kilometre plates restart at every state line
- Look for: blue vertical plates with white `km N` along a highway, usually with the road number at the top; the first plates after a state line often carry the state abbreviation (a DNIT rule of 2016; optional under CONTRAN)
- Points to: the state, and the distance from where the road enters it: kilometres are counted per state, radial roads outward from Brasília, north–south roads from north to south (except BR-156, BR-163 and BR-174), east–west roads from east to west, diagonals from north to south (except BR-307, BR-364 and BR-392). Road number + km places the photo on one stretch of road
- Strength: medium (DNIT and CONTRAN both give the restart at each state line; counting directions: single source, DNIT)
- Counterexamples: plates stop inside urban areas and resume with the urban length added; on a shared stretch the kilometres of the higher-jurisdiction road continue (federal over state; between two federal roads DNIT follows the lowest number) and the other road resumes afterwards with the shared length added; DNIT's nomenclature page omits BR-156 from its exceptions, but DNIT counts it from Laranjal do Jari in the south to Oiapoque
- Sources: DNIT, "Nomenclatura das rodovias federais" (km not cumulative between states; counting directions and exceptions), https://www.gov.br/dnit/pt-br/rodovias/rodovias-federais/nomeclatura-das-rodovias-federais; CONTRAN, Manual Brasileiro de Sinalização de Trânsito vol. III, 2022 edition, §4.1.5 (plate example BR-116 / km 181 / RJ; urban interruptions; overlaps), https://www.gov.br/transportes/pt-br/assuntos/transito/arquivos-senatran/docs/copy_of___03___MBST_Vol._III___Sin._Vert._Indicacao.pdf; DNIT, 2016 BR-Legal instruction (the first two plates after a change of state show road, km and state), https://www.gov.br/dnit/pt-br/rodovias/operacoes-rodoviarias/programa-br-legal/manual_BRLEGAL.pdf; DNIT news on BR-156 in Amapá (from Laranjal do Jari to Oiapoque), https://www.gov.br/dnit/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/dnit-e-exercito-finalizam-pavimentacao-de-mais-seis-quilometros-do-lote-4-da-br-156-ap-no-trecho-sul; pt.wikipedia "Rodovia longitudinal" (BR-156 among the south-to-north exceptions), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodovia_longitudinal

## Electricity

### Electricity distributor's name → state
- Look for: the distributor's name or logo on service vans, uniforms, customer-service shops, meter boxes and warning plates on poles or transformers; read the state in the sub-brand ("Equatorial Pará", "Energisa Tocantins", "Neoenergia Coelba"), not only the group logo
- Points to: mostly single-state companies: CEMIG → Minas Gerais; COPEL → Paraná (and Porto União, SC); CELESC → Santa Catarina (and part of Rio Negro, PR); COELBA → Bahia; COSERN → Rio Grande do Norte; Neoenergia Pernambuco → Pernambuco (and Pedras de Fogo, PB); Neoenergia Brasília → Distrito Federal; CEA → Amapá. Rio de Janeiro state is split: Light serves 31 municipalities (the city of Rio, most of the Baixada with Nova Iguaçu, Belford Roxo and São João de Meriti, Itaguaí and Seropédica, the Médio Paraíba with Volta Redonda, Barra Mansa and Valença, and the Centro-Sul with Vassouras and Três Rios) and Enel 66 by its own list: the rest of the state (Niterói, São Gonçalo, the lakes, the north and north-west, the mountain region with Petrópolis and Teresópolis, the Costa Verde with Angra dos Reis and Paraty, Resende), the four it shares with Light (Duque de Caxias, Três Rios, Paraíba do Sul, Carmo), part of Nova Friburgo (mostly Energisa) and Bocaina de Minas in Minas Gerais. Rio Grande do Sul is split between CEEE Equatorial (72 municipalities: Porto Alegre, Viamão, Alvorada, Guaíba, the whole coast from Torres to Chuí, and the south: Pelotas, Rio Grande, Bagé) and RGE (most of the interior and the northern metro: Canoas, Novo Hamburgo, Caxias do Sul, Santa Maria, Passo Fundo). Groups: Enel (also Greater São Paulo and Ceará); Equatorial (Maranhão, Pará, Piauí, Alagoas, Goiás, plus CEEE and CEA); Energisa (Acre, Rondônia, Mato Grosso, Mato Grosso do Sul, Tocantins, Paraíba, Sergipe; "Minas Rio" in Minas Gerais' Zona da Mata and Nova Friburgo; "Sul-Sudeste" in western São Paulo (Presidente Prudente, Assis), the Catanduva area, Bragança Paulista with the adjoining south of Minas (Extrema, Cambuí) and Guarapuava, PR); Neoenergia Elektro (much of inland São Paulo, much of the São Paulo coast: Ubatuba, Ilhabela, Bertioga, Guarujá, Mongaguá to Peruíbe and the Ribeira coast, and Três Lagoas, MS); CPFL Paulista (Campinas, Ribeirão Preto, Bauru); CPFL Piratininga (Santos, Sorocaba, Jundiaí); EDP (Guarulhos, Mogi das Cruzes, São José dos Campos, and Espírito Santo); Âmbar (Amazonas and Roraima)
- Strength: strong for the Neoenergia and Enel companies and CELESC; medium for the other names; a bare group logo still leaves two (EDP, Âmbar) to eleven (Energisa) states
- Counterexamples: rural electrification cooperatives with their own names serve many towns in Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina and São Paulo; Light and Enel split Duque de Caxias, Três Rios, Paraíba do Sul and Carmo; near a state line the neighbour's company appears (Porto União, Rio Negro, Pedras de Fogo above; Enel Rio in Bocaina de Minas, MG); service vans travel; ANEEL was voting on recommending forfeiture of Enel's São Paulo concession in November 2026, so the brand there may change; old imagery shows the previous brand (next entry)
- Sources: ANEEL open data, continuity indicators per consumer set and IndQual per municipality (distributor ↔ municipality), https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/indicadores-coletivos-de-continuidade-dec-e-fec and https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/indqual-municipio; ANEEL agents registry, https://dadosabertos.aneel.gov.br/dataset/agentes-do-setor-eletrico; Neoenergia, distribution (states; Pedras de Fogo), https://www.neoenergia.com/en/distribution and https://www.neoenergia.com/w/neoenergia-26-anos-atuacao-distribuicao-brasil; Enel press releases (2016 Ceará and Rio; 2018 São Paulo), https://enel.com.br/en/media/news/d201611-enel-brings-its-global-brand-to-brazil.html and https://www.enel.com.br/en/media/press/d201812-enel-brings-its-global-brand-to-so-paulo-with-enel-distribuio-so-paulo.html; Enel Rio customer summary by municipality, https://enel.com.br/content/dam/enel-br/rio-de-janeiro/Informacoes_RJ_PDF.pdf; Celesc, October 2025 (264 SC municipalities and part of Rio Negro), https://www.celesc.com.br/arquivos/candidato/2025/Outubro/Apresentacao-novos-empregados-09.10.25.pdf; Times Brasil, November 2026 (Enel SP forfeiture vote), https://timesbrasil.com.br/empresas-e-negocios/energia/aneel-marca-para-dia-17-decisao-sobre-futuro-da-enel-em-sp/; pt.wikipedia pages Neoenergia, Enel_Distribuição_Ceará, Enel_Distribuição_São_Paulo, Centrais_Elétricas_de_Santa_Catarina, Companhia_Paranaense_de_Energia, Light_S/A (31 municipalities), Grupo_Energisa (11 states), Equatorial_Energia, CPFL_Energia, EDP_Brasil

### Old distributor names date the imagery
- Look for: a distributor name that has since changed, on vans, shops, plates or poles: Eletropaulo, Ampla, Coelce, CELG or "Enel Goiás", CEEE without "Equatorial", CEMAR, AES Sul, Amazonas Energia, Roraima Energia
- Points to: the successor's area (previous entry), and imagery older than the change: Coelce (Ceará) and Ampla (Rio) became Enel in November 2016; Eletropaulo became Enel São Paulo in December 2018; CELG became Enel Goiás in 2018 and Equatorial Goiás at the end of 2022; CEMAR became Equatorial Maranhão in October 2019; CEEE passed to Equatorial in 2021; AES Sul became RGE Sul in 2016; Amazonas Energia and Roraima Energia passed to J&F's Âmbar (Roraima formalised in 2026)
- Strength: weak to medium as a date (single source per rename); the area is as strong as the previous entry
- Counterexamples: fleets, uniforms and signs are rebranded gradually; legal names lag behind brands (ANEEL's registry gives Enel RJ's legal name as Ampla Energia e Serviços and lists Enel SP under the code ELETROPAULO); names cast into concrete poles can outlast a rename by decades, since such poles are designed to last at least 35 years

### Distributor's name cast into concrete poles
- Look for: capital letters about 50 mm high cast into a concrete pole and reading along it, starting 4 m from the pole's butt (about 2–2.5 m above the ground on common 9–12 m poles), among the load (daN), length, date and manufacturer markings
- Points to: COELBA → Bahia, COSERN → Rio Grande do Norte, CELPE → Pernambuco (Neoenergia's own pole specification); a community guide adds CEMAR → Maranhão, CELPA → Pará and COELCE → Ceará

## Street-name signs

### Brasília: sector codes instead of street names
- Look for: place names built from a sector acronym and a number instead of a street name, on signs and building labels: `SQS 308`, `SQN 210`, `CLN 405`, `SCS Quadra 08 Bloco B`; in the satellite towns, quadra-and-conjunto codes such as `QR 403 Conjunto P` (Santa Maria); a block letter ("Bloco B") counts only next to such a code; blue panels identify the place, green panels give directions
- Points to: the Distrito Federal; in the Plano Piloto a final S or N means Asa Sul or Asa Norte, odd hundreds lie west of the Eixão (the north–south road axis) and even hundreds east, and the last two digits count blocks outward from the Eixo Monumental
- Strength: strong for Plano Piloto codes (SQS, SQN, CLN, SCS…); medium for a satellite-town quadra-and-conjunto code on its own
- Counterexamples: Palmas (TO) has no street names either: quadrant acronyms ending NE/NO/SE/SO (ARNE, ARNO, ARSE, ARSO; commercial ACNE, ACSO…) plus a number and an internal QI/QD, and older numbered forms such as "204 Sul" or "Quadra 504 Sul" that look like Brasília's; Cidade Ocidental (GO), 48 km from Brasília, has superquadras SQ 01–24 in its centre; the blue/green colour code was compulsory only in the Plano Piloto, Lago Norte, Lago Sul, Cruzeiro and Candangolândia (1998 decree), other RAs being free to use other designs, and the same signage model has only recently reached all 35 RAs (DER-DF contract from 2024; about 50,000 new plates reported in January 2026), so older imagery of satellite towns may show other signs

### São Paulo street-name plates
- Look for: pairs of double-faced plates at the same height on a slim metal pole at a corner, each parallel to the kerb of the street it names, so the two stand at right angles; the blue upper part has a large short name ("Brigadeiro") over the full official name ("Av. ### Plates with a subtitle, bairro or CEP line elsewhere
- Look for: extra lines under the street name (a note on the name, the bairro, the CEP or a number range) and the bands and crest around them
- Points to: Porto Alegre: concession plates since 2020 in white on blue, with the full official name, a short line on the origin of the name, the CEP and the block's number range, plus the popular name in large letters on the own-pole sets; the naming law sometimes fixes the line's wording, often about the person honoured ("Ex-morador do Bairro", "Líder comunitária local") but sometimes a slogan ("Aqui reside uma comunidade feliz"). ## Sidewalks

### São Paulo state-map mosaic
- Look for: black-and-white paving of small square pieces repeating a straight-edged outline of the state of São Paulo, in Portuguese stone or in cement tiles
- Points to: the city of São Paulo, above all its central streets (adopted in 1966)
- Strength: strong, but a single tiled frontage counts for less (the tile is sold retail)
- Counterexamples: the cement tile is sold retail for sidewalks (single source), so one tiled frontage need not be in the capital; the map is also printed on sandals, bags and other goods; many stretches are now concrete (Rua Amaral Gurgel in 2012, Avenida Paulista's stone map paving from 2007) and central removals go on with no heritage listing (2026, single source), so check the imagery date; other towns in the state were not checked
- Sources: Anais do Museu Paulista (2025: design by Mirthes Bernardes, first laid in 1966; cement-tile version from 1969; Rua Amaral Gurgel's original replaced by concrete in 2012), https://www.redalyc.org/journal/273/27383019018/27383019018.pdf; São Paulo city council magazine Apartes no. 6 (Mar–Apr 2014: three types of square piece, black and white; icon of the city; also used on sandals, bikinis and bags), https://www.saopaulo.sp.leg.br/apartes-anteriores/revista-apartes/numero-6-marco-abril2014/cobranca-justa; DCI (22 Dec 2020: 1966 contest; much of the city's paving carries the design; big brands put it on products), https://www.dci.com.br/dci-sp/conheca-a-historia-do-desenho-das-calcadas-paulistanas/65072; 360meridianos, history of Avenida Paulista (updated 29 Jun 2022: its promenades carried the stylised state design, formerly in Portuguese mosaic), https://www.360meridianos.com/historia-da-avenida-paulista/; en.wikipedia "Portuguese pavement" (seen Nov 2026: Paulista's stone sidewalks almost all replaced by concrete since 2007), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_pavement; Jornal da USP (12 Jan 2026: recent works in central São Paulo swapping stretches of the old paving for cement; Copacabana's paving is listed, the capital's is not), https://jornal.usp.br/?p=961723; Telhanorte product page (seen Nov 2026: Cimartex "Mapa São Paulo" black-and-white cement tile sold for sidewalks and other high-traffic outdoor areas), https://www.telhanorte.com.br/piso-ladrilho-hidraulico-padrao-mapa-sao-paulo-preto-e-branco-20-x-20-cm-e-caixa-com-0-48-m2-borda-reta-cimartex-2466201/p

### Curitiba pine-nut rosettes
- Look for: Portuguese-stone mosaic, black and white (rarely red), with rosettes of stylised araucária pine nuts (pinhões)
- Points to: central Curitiba: the Rua XV de Novembro pedestrian street, Praças Osório, Santos Andrade and João Cândido, and in front of the Museu Alfredo Andersen and the UFPR historic building
- Strength: strong for the motif
- Counterexamples: stone mosaic covers only 1–2% of Curitiba's sidewalks; ordinary ones are concrete pavers or slabs, often with a grass verge (verge: single source); the rosette is also printed on souvenirs; other towns in Paraná were not checked

### Black-and-white wave pavement
- Look for: Portuguese-stone pavement in alternating black and white wave bands
- Points to: Rio de Janeiro, especially the South Zone seafront from Leme to Copacabana (São Conrado repeats it); also the centre of Manaus (since 1901) and Salvador (a local wavy variant; single source)
- Strength: medium
- Counterexamples: the motif comes from Lisbon and is copied widely, in Brazil too (Curitiba sidewalks, the 18 do Forte monument in Palmas); Rio's own Portuguese pavement spread far beyond the beachfront

### Grey basalt slab sidewalks
- Look for: sidewalks of grey basalt, either irregular "crazy paving" or square unpolished slabs about 40 cm across

## Buildings

### Enxaimel (German half-timbered) houses
- Look for: an exposed timber frame with brick or clay infill, sometimes flat "German" clay roof tiles; in the countryside, houses strung along riverside roads with long narrow plots behind them running up the hill
- Points to: Santa Catarina's Itajaí valley and north (Pomerode, Blumenau, Indaial, Timbó, Jaraguá do Sul, Joinville, São Bento do Sul), plus São Bonifácio in Greater Florianópolis (single source); in Rio Grande do Sul, the old São Leopoldo colony (Ivoti, Dois Irmãos, Picada Café), the upper Taquari valley (Estrela, Teutônia, Westfália) and rural Nova Petrópolis and Gramado
- Strength: strong for a real timber frame (mostly Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul); weak for decorative façades
- Counterexamples: real frames also stand in Paraná (Curitiba area; rural Rolândia, Cambé and Warta) and in a few São Paulo city houses (Santo Amaro, Bresser), and concrete neo-enxaimel is common in urban Gramado, Campos do Jordão and Holambra and appears on two North-east buildings (Guaramiranga, Recife), all per Wikipedia (single source, uncited there); façade-only "enxaimel" ornament spread in Marechal Cândido Rondon (western Paraná) under a property-tax break (single source); Domingos Martins, Espírito Santo's first German colony, also shows enxaimel-style façades (single source)
- Sources: IPHAN Santa Catarina, preventive conservation in the immigration region, 2021 (enxaimel unusual in Brazil, built by immigrants in the South and other German-colonised regions; protected houses by municipality; lots along riverside roads; German flat tiles only in German colonisation areas), https://www.gov.br/iphan/pt-br/assuntos/noticias/iphan-lanca-material-sobre-a-preservacao-da-cultura-catarinense/conservacao-preventiva-de-imoveis-antigos-na-regiao-de-imigracao.pdf; pt.wikipedia "Enxaimel", revision of 15 Aug 2024 (towns in Santa Catarina, Rio Grande do Sul, Paraná and São Paulo; an enxaimel house in São Bonifácio; neo-enxaimel in Gramado, Campos do Jordão, Holambra and the North-east; all uncited), https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enxaimel; Jornal do Comércio, April 2026 (Ivoti has Rio Grande do Sul's largest inventoried set; Nova Petrópolis may match it), https://www.jornaldocomercio.com/jornal-cidades/2026/04/1244809-ivoti-tem-maior-conjunto-de-casas-enxaimel-inventariado-no-rs.html; Akrópolis (UNIPAR), 2003 (Marechal Cândido Rondon: a property-tax exemption led buildings to add enxaimel-style ornament to their façades), https://revistas.unipar.br/index.php/akropolis/article/download/377/343/1413; Estado de Minas, June 2026 (enxaimel-style façades in Domingos Martins), https://www.em.com.br/emfoco/?p=235770

### Wooden immigrant houses with fretwork eaves
- Look for: houses of araucária planks, often of two or more storeys, with fretwork trim (lambrequins) along eaves, verandas and gables, often with an attic; in the Italian colonies, on a stone ground floor or foundation
- Points to: the South's immigrant colonies: the Italian colonies of the Serra Gaúcha (Antônio Prado is the protected example), Polish houses around Curitiba and other wooden-house areas of Paraná, and northern Santa Catarina, where lambrequins go with the timber cycle and Polish and Ukrainian settlers (Itaiópolis, Corupá; single source); less typically, southern Santa Catarina's Italian towns (Urussanga, Orleans, Nova Veneza; single source)
- Strength: medium
- Counterexamples: lambrequins are not one group's mark: they are thought to come from plentiful timber and eclectic taste and appear among every immigrant group in Santa Catarina, so a fretwork house points to the South, not to Antônio Prado or one state; southern Santa Catarina's Italian houses share the stone cellars, low attics and lambrequins but are mostly single-storey, their finest examples in bare stone (both per IPHAN's dossier, single source)
- Sources: en.wikipedia "Antônio Prado historic center", revision of 1 Jan 2026 (araucaria wood, fretwork, many buildings of two or more storeys, 48 listed buildings), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_Prado_historic_center; MPF Rio Grande do Sul, August 2026 (48 wood-and-stone houses, largest listed ensemble of Italian immigration), https://www.mpf.mp.br/o-mpf/unidades/pr-rs/noticias/patrimonio-cultural-mpf-atua-para-preservar-legado-arquitetonico-de-antonio-prado-no-rio-grande-do-sul; FAG conference paper, 2025 (stone foundations, wooden upper floors, lambrequins on eaves), https://www4.fag.edu.br/anais-2025/artigo-resumo/influencia-italiana-na-regiao-sul-do-brasil-obras-arquitetonicas.pdf; Gazeta do Povo, February 2015 (a 1920s Curitiba wooden house with a large attic and lambrequins, an example of Paraná's popular wooden architecture), https://www.gazetadopovo.com.br/imoveis/um-exemplar-da-arquitetura-de-madeira-do-parana-ejnqxxomd0pi17m24dcci2q8e/; IPHAN, Roteiros Nacionais de Imigração – Santa Catarina dossier, c. 2007, part 5 (Urussanga, Orleans and Nova Veneza: stone, brick or wooden houses with stone cellars, low attics and wooden lambrequins, mostly single-storey, finest in bare stone), https://www.ipatrimonio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/dossi%C3%AA-imigra%C3%A7%C3%A3o-SC_volume-1-parte-5.pdf; same dossier, part 6 (lambrequins on Italian, Ukrainian and above all Polish buildings, on verandas and gables, probably owed to plentiful timber and eclecticism; Antônio Prado, Polish houses around Curitiba, northern Santa Catarina's timber region), https://www.ipatrimonio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/dossi%C3%AA-imigra%C3%A7%C3%A3o-SC_volume-1-parte-6.pdf; same dossier, part 7 (examples in Itaiópolis and Corupá; found among all groups), https://www.ipatrimonio.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/dossi%C3%AA-imigra%C3%A7%C3%A3o-SC_volume-1-parte-7.pdf

### Façades clad in glazed tiles (azulejos)
- Look for: colonial townhouses built flush with the street whose whole façades are covered in patterned glazed tiles, on cobbled streets
- Points to: São Luís (Maranhão), which has the largest set of tiled façades in Brazil; a few tiled sobrados also survive in Alcântara, on São Marcos Bay (single source)
- Strength: medium (São Luís is the likeliest match, but Belém also keeps many tiled façades)
- Counterexamples: Belém (Pará) has one of Brazil's most varied sets of tiled façades, still kept on old mansions in Cidade Velha, Campina and Reduto; outside Brazil, Lisbon and Porto also have tiled façades
- Sources: en.wikipedia "Historic Centre of São Luís", revision of 26 Jul 2026 (townhouses flush with the street; largest tile collection in Brazil), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Centre_of_S%C3%A3o_Lu%C3%ADs; IFMA paper in Caderno PPGD/UFAM, 2024 (177 tiled façades in a 1986 survey; façade tiling also taken up in Lisbon and Porto), https://periodicos.ufam.edu.br/index.php/cadernoppgd/article/download/16422/9991/43603; O Imparcial, September 2016 ("city of tiles"; tiled houses on cobbled streets), https://oimparcial.com.br/cidades/2016/09/os-preciosos-palacios-de-porcelana/; Museu Histórico de Alcântara (Ibram), illustrated town map, 2022 (two sobrados with tiled façades in Alcântara), https://museuhistoricodealcantara.museus.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/MAPA-alcantara-A2-frente-e-verso-0704-compactado.pdf; Sanjad et al. in Pós (UFMG), November 2014 (Belém's tiled façades among Brazil's most representative sets, with Portuguese, German, French, English and Dutch tiles), https://periodicos.ufmg.br/index.php/revistapos/article/download/15484/12340/42960; Diário do Pará, April 2025 (mansions in Reduto, Campina and Cidade Velha still keep tiled façades and verandas), https://diariodopara.com.br/noticias/a-pedra-polida-que-e-um-simbolo-brasil-portugal/

### Wattle-and-daub (taipa) houses
- Look for: small houses whose walls show a lattice of sticks packed with mud, often unplastered; common in dry scrub country but not limited to it
- Points to: the rural North-east and parts of the North: the highest shares of residents in unplastered taipa are in interior Maranhão (Marajá do Sena, Serrano do Maranhão, Aldeias Altas, Jenipapo dos Vieiras, none on SUDENE's semi-arid list) and in Uiramutã, Roraima (single source); also the sertão of Ceará, Paraíba, Sergipe and neighbours
- Strength: medium
- Counterexamples: the technique exists in other regions under other names; housing programmes are replacing these houses, so newer imagery shows fewer
- Sources: UFMG master's thesis on taipa in semi-arid Sergipe, 2022 (still frequent in the North-east, found through Street View), https://repositorio.ufmg.br/bitstream/1843/51731/1/Arquitetura%20vern%c3%a1cula%20e%20popular_a%20taipa%20de%20m%c3%a3o%20no%20semi%c3%a1rido%20sergipano.pdf; Diário do Nordeste, 2021 (about 76,000 inadequate homes in Ceará, almost all taipa, mostly rural), https://diariodonordeste.verdesmares.com.br/regiao/problema-da-moradia-inadequada-no-sertao-e-exposto-com-mais-de-75-mil-casas-de-taipa-1.3122691; A União, 2026 (taipa houses replaced in 13 sertão towns of Paraíba), https://auniao.pb.gov.br/noticias/caderno_paraiba/casas-de-taipa-sao-substituidas-por-novas-moradias-no-sertao-do-estado; IBGE, Censo 2022 household characteristics, 2024 (unplastered taipa was home to 0.6% of Brazilians; Maranhão has the highest state share, 6.6%; 8 of the 9 municipalities above 25% are in Maranhão), https://biblioteca.ibge.gov.br/visualizacao/livros/liv102150.pdf; Paraíba Business, December 2024 (Uiramutã highest at 60%, then Marajá do Sena at 35% and other Maranhão towns at 26–28%), https://paraibabusiness.com.br/mais-de-dois-milhoes-de-brasileiros-ainda-v/; SUDENE, CONDEL resolution 150, December 2021 (official semi-arid list, which leaves out those Maranhão towns), https://www.gov.br/sudene/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/resolucaocondel1502021.pdf/@@display-file/file

### Stilt houses (palafitas)
- Look for: wooden houses on stakes over water or a flood plain, linked by plank walkways
- Points to: Amazon riverbanks and wetlands (Belém, Macapá), but not only there
- Strength: weak (boost only; needs the vegetation and river context)
- Counterexamples: Brazil's largest stilt-house favela, the Dique da Vila Gilda in Santos (São Paulo), was still there in 2026, and neighbouring São Vicente has several more; others stand in the Baixada Fluminense and the Pantanal (single source)
