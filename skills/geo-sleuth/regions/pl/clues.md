# Poland clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Poland is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-11-03. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and plates

### Polish words with diacritics on fixed signs
- Look for: Polish on shop fronts, station boards and official signs: `Wyjście` (exit), `Wejście` (entrance), `Dworzec` (station), `Apteka`, `Urząd` (office), `Otwarte`/`Zamknięte`, and the characteristic `ł`, `ą`, `ę`, `ż`, `ź` letters.
- Points to: Poland; the diacritics are the strongest country-level text clue.
- Strength: strong for the country; weak for the region, since standard Polish is uniform.
- Counterexamples: Lithuanian and Czech also use Latin script with diacritics, but different letters (`č`, `ř`, `ė`, `ų`). Slovak shares some letters but not the full set.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Polish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) describes the alphabet and distribution.

### Two or three letters then digits, with a voivodeship prefix
- Look for: a white plate with black characters and a blue EU band with `PL`, in the format `XX 12345` or `XX 1234A`, where the first one or two letters name the voivodeship (`W` Mazowieckie, `K` Małopolskie, `S` Śląskie, `D` Dolnośląskie, `P` Wielkopolskie, `G` Pomorskie).
- Points to: the voivodeship and county of registration; the leading letters are the county code.
- Strength: medium (single source); the plate confirms Poland and narrows the region.
- Counterexamples: the code is where the vehicle was registered, not where the photo was taken, and a rental can carry a plate from another region. The full county table has several hundred codes.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Poland) lists the voivodeship prefixes.

## Addresses and numbers

### Postal code as `XX-XXX`
- Look for: a five-digit code written with a hyphen after the second digit, for example `00-001 Warszawa`, `31-001 Kraków` or `80-001 Gdańsk`, on mailboxes, forms and shop windows.
- Points to: Poland; the `XX-XXX` shape with the hyphen is the clearest postal marker in Europe.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the region, since the first digits group the country.
- Counterexamples: other countries write five digits without a hyphen (`10115 Berlin`), or with different group sizes. A code can be printed on a mail piece handled elsewhere.
- Sources: [Poczta Polska](https://www.poczta-polska.pl/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Nine digits with `+48`
- Look for: phone numbers written as nine digits (`22` Warsaw, `12` Kraków, `58` Gdańsk, `61` Poznań) with no leading zero, and mobiles starting `5`, `6`, `7` or `8`, or the country code `+48` abroad.
- Points to: Poland; the nine-digit, no-trunk-zero format is Polish.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: the Czech Republic and Slovakia also use nine digits, but with `+420`/`+421` and different prefixes; only the country code or the specific area code separates them.
- Sources: [UKE](https://www.uke.gov.pl/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`S`/`DK` network
- Look for: blue direction signs, `A` motorway numbers (`A1`, `A2`, `A4`), `S` expressway numbers (`S7`, `S8`), `DK` national road numbers, and `Objazd` diversion signs.
- Points to: Poland; the `A`/`S`/`DK` hierarchy with blue signs is Polish.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the region.
- Counterexamples: Germany and the Czech Republic also use blue motorway signs and `A` numbers; a route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [GDDKiA](https://www.gov.pl/web/gddkia) manages the national roads; [Wikipedia: Highways in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Poland) lists the network.

### PKP trains and the discount chains
- Look for: `PKP Intercity` or `Polregio` rail vehicles and station signage, or the chains `Biedronka`, `Żabka`, `Lidl` and `Rossmann`.
- Points to: Poland; the PKP brands and the `Biedronka`/`Żabka` chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the region.
- Counterexamples: Lidl and Rossmann operate across Europe under the same brands, and PKP runs cross-border services to Germany, the Czech Republic, Slovakia and Lithuania.
- Sources: [PKP Intercity](https://www.intercity.pl/) publishes its network; [Biedronka](https://www.biedronka.pl/) lists its stores.
