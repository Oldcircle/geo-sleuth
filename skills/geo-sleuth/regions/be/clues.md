# Belgium clues

Entries use the shared format (`references/clues/README.md`). Use these only after Belgium is a candidate; cross-country clues belong in `references/clues/global.md`. Sources checked on 2026-10-31. The pack is a stub: clues only, no admin table or lookup tables yet.

## Text and signs

### Two or three languages on one sign
- Look for: Dutch and French side by side on the same sign, for example `Bruxelles/Brussel`, `Sortie/Uitgang`, `Gare/Station`, or a triple `Luik/Liège/Lüttich` in the east.
- Points to: Belgium; the bilingual pair on official signs is the clearest country-level marker, and the language mix narrows the region.
- Strength: strong for the country; medium for the region (Dutch in Flanders, French in Wallonia, German around Eupen, both in Brussels).
- Counterexamples: Canada and Switzerland also print two languages, but with different languages; a single-language sign in Flanders or Wallonia is normal, so one word does not separate the regions.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Languages of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Belgium) describes the language areas; [belgium.be](https://www.belgium.be/) is the federal portal.

### White plate with red characters
- Look for: a white plate with red characters, a blue band with the EU stars and `B` on the left, and the format `1-ABC-123` (since 2010), with no region code.
- Points to: Belgium as the issuing country; the red lettering separates it from the Netherlands' black-on-yellow and France's black-on-white plates.
- Strength: strong for the country (single source); useless for a province.
- Counterexamples: older Belgian plates used other layouts, and Luxembourg uses yellow plates with black text. A rental car with a foreign plate says nothing about the photo's location.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Vehicle registration plates of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Belgium) documents the current series.

## Addresses and numbers

### Four digits before the town name
- Look for: a four-digit postal code written before the town, for example `1000 Bruxelles`, `2000 Antwerpen` or `4000 Liège`, on mailboxes, forms and shop windows.
- Points to: Belgium; the first digit groups the country, with `1xxx` around Brussels and `8xxx`-`9xxx` in West and East Flanders.
- Strength: medium (single source); the code names a delivery area, not a building.
- Counterexamples: the Netherlands also uses four digits but adds two letters (`1234 AB`); Germany uses five digits. A code can be printed on a mail piece handled elsewhere.
- Sources: [bpost](https://www.bpost.be/) publishes the postal-code directory and format.

### Area codes with a leading zero, and `+32`
- Look for: phone numbers written with a leading zero (`02` Brussels, `03` Antwerp, `04` Liège, `09` Ghent) and mobile numbers starting `04`, or the country code `+32` abroad.
- Points to: Belgium; the leading zero is dropped only with the country code.
- Strength: medium (single source).
- Counterexamples: the Netherlands also uses a leading zero, but with different city codes (`020` Amsterdam, `010` Rotterdam); only the `+32` or the specific code separates them.
- Sources: [BIPT](https://www.bipt.be/) is the regulator that publishes the numbering plan.

## Roads and services

### Blue motorway signs and the `A`/`E`/`R` network
- Look for: blue direction signs, `A` motorway numbers (`A1`, `A2`, `A7`), European `E` numbers, and the `R0` ring around Brussels.
- Points to: Belgium; the dense `A` network with short numbers and the `R0` ring are Belgian.
- Strength: medium; a single sign confirms the country, not the province.
- Counterexamples: France and the Netherlands also use `A` numbers and blue signs, and `E` numbers cross borders. A route number can appear on a map photographed elsewhere.
- Sources: [Wikipedia: Motorways of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways_of_Belgium) lists the network; [SPF Mobilité](https://mobilit.belgium.be/) is the federal transport authority.

### NMBS/SNCB trains and the supermarket chains
- Look for: rail vehicles and station signs marked `NMBS/SNCB` (sometimes `SNCB/NMBS`), or the chains `Delhaize`, `Colruyt` and `Aldi` on shop fronts and delivery vans.
- Points to: Belgium; the bilingual rail brand and the Belgian grocery chains are specific to the country.
- Strength: medium (single source); the brands confirm the country but not the province.
- Counterexamples: Delhaize also operates in Luxembourg and the United States under other names, and SNCB trains run to France and the Netherlands. Franchise holders can use the brand outside its home market.
- Sources: [SNCB/NMBS](https://www.belgiantrain.be/) is the national rail operator; [Delhaize](https://www.delhaize.be/) and [Colruyt](https://www.colruyt.be/) publish their store networks.
